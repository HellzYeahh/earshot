# Android plumbing, not standards, makes Earshot universal

No Bluetooth standard and no Google channel will let a third-party app switch noise cancelling across brands. Earshot's "universal remote" therefore has two layers: Android platform features that now work on every headset, and vendor drivers picked for speed. The Bluetooth SIG has no ANC or transparency specification, adopted, drafted or announced. Google Fast Pair lets any app *read* ANC state, but it reserves *Set ANC* for Google Play services through an account-key MAC, and only Pixel Buds Pro are confirmed to implement those messages. Android has also blocked app access to LE Audio volume, AICS and hearing-aid presets since Android 13. The biggest accelerator is the Companion Device Manager (CDM). One association per headset lets Earshot connect and disconnect any headset on Android 17, rename it (API 31), unpair it (API 36) and be woken when it is present. That turns roadmap item F4 from Play hygiene into the core of the "Any headset" tier. Two roadmap assumptions break:
- **The D3 desk answer is wrong.** `createUsingSocketSettings` rejects classic L2CAP, so AirPods modes (C2) need a blocklisted hidden-API bypass even on Android 17.
- **E4 shrinks.** Play services almost certainly holds the Fast Pair Message Stream, and over-ear headphones don't send battery over it at all.

The competitive slot is open. No Android app pairs multi-brand drivers with system-wide audio and call features. The closest rival, Gadgetbridge, isn't on Play. The OEM apps Earshot replaces carry up to 8 trackers and 102 permissions.

## No standard switches ANC, so Fast Pair shrinks to battery and identity

The SIG's adopted list covers every audio profile from A2DP to VOCS, and none of them touches noise control. Its in-development page highlights lossless audio, spatial audio, multichannel, voice assistants, sound-exposure reporting and Auracast; **ANC, transparency and ambient modes are absent** ([SIG specifications](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/); [SIG in development](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/in-development/)). The nearest standard piece is the AICS "Ambient" input type. Android 16 Settings uses it for hearing-aid ambient volume, but only through a `@SystemApi` that needs `BLUETOOTH_PRIVILEGED` ([AmbientVolumeController](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/android16-release/packages/SettingsLib/src/com/android/settingslib/bluetooth/AmbientVolumeController.java#40)). Apps can't go around that with their own GATT client either. **Since Android 13 the GATT service marks every handle of VCS, VOCS, AICS, PACS, ASCS, BASS, HAS and CSIS as restricted.** The services still appear in discovery, but reads and writes need `BLUETOOTH_PRIVILEGED`, and the list is unchanged in `android17-release` ([android13 GattService](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/android13-release/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/gatt/GattService.java#168); [android17 GattUtil.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/android17-release/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/gatt/GattUtil.kt#47)). Spatial audio is read-only for apps: every setter, including recentre and head-tracking mode, is privileged, and the head-tracker sensor is limited to audioserver and system_server ([Spatializer.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/media/java/android/media/Spatializer.java#129); [SensorService.cpp](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/native/+/refs/heads/main/services/sensorservice/SensorService.cpp#2294)). Earshot can show "spatial audio on, head tracker available" and link to system settings, and no more.

Fast Pair looked like the cross-brand shortcut, and the landscape doc rated it "battery, ring, ANC read". Without an account key, an app can read L/R/case battery with a charging bit, remaining time, worn state, model ID, firmware and the current ANC mode ([Device information](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/deviceinformation); [Hearable Controls](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/hearablecontrols)). **Set ANC and every Audio Switch write need an HMAC keyed by an account key.** The spec says the MAC exists so the headset can confirm the sender is GMSCore and "not another app" ([Fast Pair MAC](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/mac)). Earshot can't put its own key on the headset either. That needs each model's anti-spoofing key from Google's online registry, and on a headset never paired to Android the first key becomes the Find Hub owner key. The WhisperPair researchers showed how that ownership can be abused ([WhisperPair](https://whisperpair.eu/)). Even the read side is narrow. Hearable Controls is optional, earbuds-only, switched on per model in Google's console, and not listed for over-ear headphones. Over-ears also get **no battery notification** under the certification rules ([Hearables requirements](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/devicefeaturerequirement/devicefeaturerequirement_hearables)). The only confirmed ANC implementers are Pixel Buds Pro and Pro 2 ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/android-15-might-return-anc-controls-to-the-volume-panel_id161781)).

The channel itself is very likely taken. The phone opens the Message Stream as soon as the headset connects, including headsets paired through ordinary Settings ([Message Stream](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/messagestream); [Retroactive account key](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/retroactiveacctkey)). **AOSP's RFCOMM layer refuses a second client on the same channel and device with `PORT_ALREADY_OPENED`** ([AOSP port_api.cc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/system/stack/rfcomm/port_api.cc)). The only third-party client found is an unmerged BlueZ patch on Linux that reads Pixel Buds Pro battery ([linux-bluetooth thread](https://ratatoskr.run/linux-bluetooth/2026/08/17446083/t)); no report exists for an Android app either way. Spike D5 is still worth an hour on the soundcore Boom 2, the one device here known to advertise `df21fe2c` ([roadmap](../../roadmap.md)), but plan for "fails while Play services runs".

Three things survive:
- **Identification.** A `df21fe2c` entry in SDP identifies a Fast Pair device cheaply, which helps A2.
- **An advert battery snapshot.** Non-discoverable adverts carry optional battery but no model ID, and the spec tells makers not to include battery all the time ([Battery Notification](https://developers.google.com/nearby/fast-pair/specifications/extensions/batterynotification)). That gives a snapshot around case-open that can only be matched to your headset heuristically.
- **A clean licence.** The spec is CC-BY and Google's own Chromium parser is BSD-3, so it can be ported with credit ([fast_pair_data_parser.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/chromeos/ash/services/quick_pair/fast_pair_data_parser.cc)).

Turning a model ID into a product name needs Google's online Nearby API, so that part is off-limits to Earshot ([device_metadata_fetcher.cc](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/ash/quick_pair/repository/fast_pair/device_metadata_fetcher.cc)).

## Android 16 and 17 give every headset a remote through CDM

The Companion Device Manager now goes well beyond presence callbacks.
- **Connect and disconnect (API 37).** `BluetoothDevice.connect()` "will page the device before initiating profile connections", and `disconnect()` comes with it. Both need `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` plus either `BLUETOOTH_PRIVILEGED` or a CDM association ([BluetoothDevice.connect](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothDevice#connect())).
- **Rename (API 31).** `setAlias` renames the headset on the phone and survives reboots ([setAlias](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothDevice#setAlias(java.lang.String))).
- **Unpair (API 36).** `CompanionDeviceManager.removeBond` ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)).
- **Presence (API 36).** `onDevicePresenceEvent` binds the app on connect and raises its process priority ([Android 16 features](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/features); [CompanionDeviceService](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/companion/CompanionDeviceService)).
- **No re-pairing.** A single-device request with a MAC filter also searches bonded devices, so associating an already-paired headset costs one system dialog ([AssociationRequest](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/companion/AssociationRequest)).

Together these give **"switch to this headset", "disconnect", "rename" and "forget" for any brand, with no driver and no reflection**. That covers the multipoint and switching problems Sony and Bose users complain about.

Earshot compiles against SDK 36 (`app/build.gradle.kts`), so the API 37 calls need `compileSdk 37` from the integrator. Codec control stays the open question. `getCodecStatus` and `setCodecConfigPreference` accept a CDM association in place of the privileged permission, but they are still `@SystemApi @hide` ([AOSP BluetoothA2dp.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/framework/java/android/bluetooth/BluetoothA2dp.java)). D4 found them flagged "sdk,system-api", which reflection may call ([roadmap](../../roadmap.md)). So codec switching without Shizuku, through reflection plus an association, remains F4's most valuable untested experiment. Also untested: whether `connect()` takes a multipoint headset away from another phone, and how OEM stacks behave.

Three standard GATT services stay open to apps' own GATT clients:
- **BAS (`0x180F`), DIS (`0x180A`) and MICS (`0x184D`, Mute `0x2BC3`)** appear on no restricted list from Android 12 to 17 ([AOSP GattService main](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/gatt/GattService.java#97); [Zephyr UUIDs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/main/include/zephyr/bluetooth/uuid.h)).
- **BAS gives a Shizuku-free starting battery value.** Android's own BAS client reads it through the public `connectGatt` ([BatteryStateMachine](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/bas/BatteryStateMachine.java#60)), so Earshot can do the same on any LE-capable headset. The QC Ultra 2 is one: it advertises BAS, VCS and BASS ([roadmap](../../roadmap.md)).
- **MICS corrects the landscape doc,** which listed Microphone Control as unavailable. AOSP ships no MICP client, so nothing protects the mic-mute characteristic ([AOSP Bluetooth app tree](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/)). How many headsets expose a writable Mute is unknown.

One caveat: on Android 13–16 an app's LE GATT connection could, in theory, nudge LE Audio auto-activation. Android 17 adds logic to stop app GATT connections "hijacking an active LE Audio stream" ([android17 GattService](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/android17-release/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/gatt/GattService.java#1134)).

Three more cheap universal features fall out of public APIs.
- **Find my earbuds.** A one-sided stereo `AudioTrack` routed to the headset needs no vendor protocol. It must start from the UI, tile, widget or a notification, because Android 17 silences background playback without a qualifying foreground service ([background audio hardening](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/changes/bg-audio)). This meets find/ring demand, one of CAPod's three most-upvoted issues and an OpenSCQ30 request, without Fast Pair ([CAPod issues](https://github.com/d4rken-org/capod/issues); [OpenSCQ30 issues](https://github.com/Oppzippy/OpenSCQ30/issues)). Untested: the mono-audio and balance accessibility settings may defeat a one-sided tone, and a bud in its case may not play at all.
- **A connection diagnostics timeline.** It can show the reason for each failure: A2DP and HFP disconnect reasons (API 37), bond-loss intents open to `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` since Android 16, encryption changes and the cached LE Appearance (version 37.2) ([BluetoothA2dp](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothA2dp); [BluetoothDevice](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothDevice)).
- **A call-codec label.** `BluetoothHeadset.getCodecType` (version 37.2) tells CVSD, mSBC and LC3 super-wideband calls apart with only `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` ([BluetoothHeadset](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothHeadset#getCodecType(android.bluetooth.BluetoothDevice))). It fits next to call boost and mic check.

For B1's battery sources, the HFP vendor channel works in one direction only:
- **Headset to phone:** only an allow-list of commands is broadcast: `+XEVENT`, `+ANDROID`, `+CGM*`, `+XAPL` and `+IPHONEACCEV`.
- **Phone to headset:** only `+ANDROID` can be sent ([HeadsetService](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/hfp/HeadsetService.java#2138)).
- **The intent category uses the decimal company ID**, `.85` for Plantronics rather than the javadoc's `.55` ([HeadsetStateMachine](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/hfp/HeadsetStateMachine.java#1880)).

Earshot's remapper design is confirmed. AVRCP play/pause/next presses never become input events: the stack hands them straight to `MediaSessionManager.dispatchMediaKeyEvent` ([MediaPlayerList.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/android/app/src/com/android/bluetooth/audio_util/MediaPlayerList.java)). Key Mapper's own FAQ admits many Bluetooth headphones "aren't supported" ([Key Mapper FAQ](https://github.com/keymapperorg/KeyMapperDocs/blob/develop/docs/faq.md)). Adding an AccessibilityService would bring Play declarations and gain nothing for Bluetooth headphones. Android 17's Advanced Protection mode also blocks non-tool accessibility services ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/android-17-advanced-protection-locks.html)).

## AirPods modes need a hidden-API bypass, in a niche LibrePods already owns

The roadmap's D3 note says `createUsingSocketSettings` with `TYPE_L2CAP = 3` is public. It isn't usable. `setSocketType` "must be one of TYPE_RFCOMM, TYPE_LE", `setL2capPsm` accepts only LE PSMs 128–255, and the AOSP builder throws `IllegalArgumentException("invalid socketType")` for anything else ([Builder reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/bluetooth/BluetoothSocketSettings.Builder); [AOSP BluetoothSocketSettings.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/packages/modules/Bluetooth/+/refs/heads/main/framework/java/android/bluetooth/BluetoothSocketSettings.java)). **Android 17 added no public way to open classic L2CAP.**

The widely reported "AirPods fix" is a Bluetooth-stack change to how the ERTM channel mode is negotiated (Google issue 371713238). It shipped on Pixel with Android 16 QPR3 plus a Play system update and on ColorOS/OxygenOS 16, and is expected on all Android 17 devices ([LibrePods Android README](https://github.com/kavishdevar/librepods/blob/main/android/README.md)). Whether One UI 9 includes it is unknown. LibrePods' Play build reaches PSM `0x1001` by reflecting on `BluetoothSocket`'s private constructor, after a JNI `VMRuntime.setHiddenApiExemptions` call ([LibrePods bluetooth_socket.cpp](https://github.com/kavishdevar/librepods/blob/main/android/app/src/main/cpp/bluetooth_socket.cpp)). Google keeps tightening reflection: apps targeting Android 17 can no longer change static final fields ([Android 17 behaviour changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17)).

The market makes the cost harder to justify. **LibrePods has 30.4k GitHub stars, a 4.8★ Play listing with 50K+ installs and a €5 unlock**, and CAPod has 500K+ installs ([LibrePods GitHub](https://github.com/librepods-org/librepods); [LibrePods Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=me.kavishdevar.librepods); [CAPod Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=eu.darken.capod)). Only one app can hold the AAP channel. C2 would cost a clean-room effort built on our own HCI captures, ride on a bypass Google can close in any release, and compete head-on with a well-loved incumbent.

The better default is an AirPods tier made of C1's advert battery plus Earshot's any-headset features, running alongside LibrePods and reporting it through the existing `ChannelHeld` problem. Modes become a go/no-go decision after a One UI 9 test. The Bose core is unaffected: `createInsecureRfcommSocket(int)` is on the allowed "unsupported" list ([landscape](../headset-landscape-2026-10.md)).

## Brand breadth comes from shared codecs and neglected OEM apps

Qualcomm GAIA v3 has real standard features, but few brands use them. Open code shows a discovery command (Basic `0x0001` returns feature/version pairs) and two standard features:
- **Audio Curation (feature 8):** mode count, get and set mode, change notifications.
- **Music Processing (feature 5):** presets and a user EQ.

([moondrop-control PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/FEAKEuser/moondrop-control/blob/HEAD/docs/PROTOCOL.md); [Gadgetbridge EarFunPacket.java](https://codeberg.org/Freeyourgadget/Gadgetbridge/src/branch/master/app/src/main/java/nodomain/freeyourgadget/gadgetbridge/service/devices/earfun/EarFunPacket.java))

**Only Moondrop is shown driving them directly.** EarFun advertises both features but sets modes through its own `0x000A` commands. Sennheiser uses vendor ID `0x0495`, vivo uses `0x001B`, and SoundPEATS uses GAIA v2 `0x03xx` commands ([momentumctl](https://codeberg.org/galen/momentumctl); [HyperEars vivo doc](https://github.com/silverpoetry/HyperEars/blob/HEAD/docs/vivo-tws-air3-pro-protocol.md); [budsctl](https://github.com/hammad-iftikhar/budsctl)). Firmware quirks add work: mode values are read as an index but written as 1/2/4, SET commands are never acknowledged, and the firmware ignores everything for about 2 s after a mode change.

So the accelerator is a **shared GAIA codec in `:protocol`**, not a generic driver:
- framing and the feature probe;
- a serial request queue;
- read-back verification after each write;
- an allow-list WriteGuard: never feature 6 (Upgrade), never the v1/v2 groups `0x01xx`, `0x02xx`, `0x06xx` and `0x07xx`, and never EarFun's `0x0305` reset or `0x0308` reboot;
- thin per-brand tables on top.

Two corrections to the landscape doc: SpaceTravel-Protocol's feature table conflicts with hardware-tested sources, so use it for framing only. Qualcomm's own manual is login-gated, and GitHub copies of "GAIA Control" carry Qualcomm copyright with no licence grant ([GAIAControl copy](https://github.com/qiu-yongheng/GAIAControl)). We own no GAIA headset, so this belongs in 0.8, driven by device reports.

Demand evidence points to which brands to pick. Device requests dominate every open-source tracker ([OpenSCQ30 issues](https://github.com/Oppzippy/OpenSCQ30/issues)), and OEM app quality is a usable targeting signal.
- **Sennheiser.** Smart Control sits at **2.4★ and was last updated 2025-05-28** ([Sennheiser Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sennheiser.control)). An MIT reference covers ANC strength, adaptive ANC, anti-wind and EQ on the Momentum 4/5 ([momentum4-control](https://github.com/Ariazonaa/momentum4-control)). That makes Sennheiser the strongest Experimental candidate after Sony.
- **Pixel Buds Pro.** The Pixel Buds app holds 2.6★ ([Pixel Buds Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.wearables.maestro.companion)), and the reference is already MIT.
- **Bose QC35 (E5).** Gadgetbridge already supports the NC 700 and QC35, and it added quick-settings tiles for seven brands between August and October 2026 ([Gadgetbridge Bose](https://gadgetbridge.org/gadgets/headphones/bose/); [Gadgetbridge releases](https://codeberg.org/Freeyourgadget/Gadgetbridge/releases)). E5 is no longer unique. Earshot's moat stays QC Ultra/QC45 depth, Play presence and the call and audio features.
- **Per-headset EQ (B3).** Wavelet's **5M+ installs** confirm the demand, and its conflict with other `DynamicsProcessing` apps means Earshot should say how it coexists with them ([Wavelet Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pittvandewitt.wavelet); [RootlessJamesDSP](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/me.timschneeberger.rootlessjamesdsp/)).
- **Codec panel.** Bluetooth Codec Changer (500K+, per-app profiles) means the panel is not unique on features ([Codec Changer Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.amrg.bluetooth_codec_converter)).

Store copy can make the privacy contrast checkable: Bose ships 8 trackers, Nothing X requests 92 permissions and Samsung's Buds plugin 102 ([Exodus Bose](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/com.bose.bosemusic/latest/); [Exodus Nothing X](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/com.nothing.smartcenter/latest/); [Exodus Samsung](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/com.samsung.accessory.budsunitemgr/latest/)). Distribution lessons from comparable apps:
- a separate `.foss` application ID, after LibrePods users saw Play "update" a GitHub build ([LibrePods issues](https://github.com/librepods-org/librepods/issues));
- an icon and store-asset carve-out from the licence, as CAPod does ([CAPod README](https://github.com/d4rken-org/capod));
- developer verification, already enforced in four countries since 2026-09-30 ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/12067/keep-android-open-google-developer-verification-sideloading)).

## Conclusion

Universality on the headset side is closed by design. Google's MAC keeps writes for its own client, and AOSP's restricted handle list blocks app access to LE Audio services. What Earshot can claim honestly lives on the phone side: every headset gets connect, rename, find, diagnostics, EQ and call tools, and named drivers add deep control on top. The 1.0 headline should promise exactly that layering rather than "controls any headset". The single-holder rule Earshot already lives with for BMAP now applies to Google too: Play services holds the Fast Pair stream the same way the Bose app holds BMAP.

These findings also add a driver-selection criterion the roadmap lacks: **transport risk**. Earshot's Bose core runs on an *allowed* hidden API. Sony (RFCOMM by SDP UUID), Pixel Buds (RFCOMM by UUID), Nothing (SPP UUID) and Sennheiser (GAIA over RFCOMM or BLE) are reachable through public socket APIs. AirPods are the outlier, because classic L2CAP needs a *blocklisted* bypass. Ranking vendors by transport risk, MIT reference availability and how badly users rate the OEM app produces a faster and safer 0.7/0.8 order than ranking by market share.
